# Getting a good CIS session

Read this when the user asks how to prepare for a session, whether one is worth running, or what to do with the result.

## When a session is the wrong tool

- `bmad-cis-design-thinking`: a purely technical problem no person touches, backend or infrastructure work, or no chance to put anything in front of users.
- `bmad-cis-innovation-strategy`: an incremental feature improvement, a technical optimization, or a tactical question such as how to beat one competitor this quarter. For feature ideas suggest `bmad-brainstorming` when it is installed.
- `bmad-cis-problem-solving`: a well-understood problem with an obvious fix, a small isolated bug, or an emergency that needs action now. Run it afterwards to find why it happened.
- `bmad-cis-storytelling`: technical documentation, plain factual reporting, or a headline or tagline.

## How to open one

The first answer sets the quality of the session. Help the user frame it before they start.

- Design thinking: frame the challenge around a person and a need, not a solution. "Help small business owners understand their cash flow" works. "Build a new dashboard" starts from the answer.
- Innovation strategy: ask a strategic question. "What business models could work for this idea?" works. "What features should we build?" is product planning.
- Problem solving: describe symptoms with specifics. "Checkout is abandoned 40% of the time after payment details are entered" works. "Checkout is broken" does not. Say what was already tried.
- Storytelling: state the purpose and the audience. "Persuade investors the market is large" works. "Tell our company story" is too vague to pick a framework.

## What to bring

A session can start from a document: user research or interview notes, a market analysis, a problem brief or incident notes, brand guidelines. Tell the user to name it when they start. Informal notes help. Without one the session works from what the user says.

## What to expect

Design thinking and problem solving hold the user in understanding before any solution, and every session pushes for many options before choosing. That is the method, not a delay. In design thinking a negative test result is a good outcome.

## What can follow

No session leads to another, but these pairings help when the user asks what next.

- After design thinking: innovation strategy to check the business case, storytelling to tell the user's story, brainstorming from Maya's menu when more ideas are needed.
- After innovation strategy: design thinking to shape the experience, storytelling for the strategic narrative, Caravaggio to plan the deck.
- After problem solving: design thinking when users feel the problem, innovation strategy when the fix is a strategic change.
- After storytelling: Caravaggio to plan the deck or find the visual, and to critique it once built.
- After any of them, when a product or a change is to be built: the session document is input to any planning skill from another installed module.
